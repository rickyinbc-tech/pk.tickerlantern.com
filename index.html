03 / YOUR BANKROLL

Kelly criterion: how much of a bankroll to risk

Teaching tool

The Kelly criterion sizes a repeatable bet with fixed odds to grow a bankroll as fast as possible over the long run. Here it helps you see how much of your poker bankroll a bet exposes. It does not choose a bet size or a call in a poker hand.

What Kelly assumes, and why poker breaks it

  • Kelly’s setting: the same bet repeated many times, independent outcomes, a known probability p, a fixed payout b, any stake you like, and a loss of exactly the stake when you lose. It maximizes the long-run growth rate (expected logarithm of the bankroll), not profit on a single bet.
  • Fractional Kelly: for small edges, half Kelly often retains roughly three-quarters of the expected log growth with a smaller stake. Overbetting beyond full Kelly lowers expected log growth and increases risk; the results depend on the odds and probability.
  • Estimates: Kelly treats p as known. Overestimating p can lead to overbetting and substantial losses. That is a common reason to use half or quarter Kelly.
  • Equity is not a win probability: showdown equity mixes outright wins with split-pot shares and assumes the hand reaches showdown. A tie is not a win, and a split pot is not a returned stake.
  • Poker odds are not fixed: changing a bet changes who calls or folds, what range continues and how much you win. Rake, side pots and later streets change the payout too, and hands are not identical repeats.
  • A call is a fixed price: you can call or fold, not call part of a bet. Kelly can show how much of your bankroll that call exposes; it cannot choose the call amount.

Primary source: J. L. Kelly Jr., “A New Interpretation of Information Rate,” Bell System Technical Journal 35(4), 1956, pp. 917–926. Wiley Online Library
